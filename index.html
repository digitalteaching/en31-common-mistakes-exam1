The summary is in the simple present

in three out of four exams Many summaries told the story like a story: in the past.

How it works

  1. The plot of the text: simple present (drinks, sees, decide)
  2. No progressive form: not are taking, but take
  3. Before the plot starts: present perfect (has invented)
  4. Irregular verbs: check the form (sell – sold, sleep – slept, beat – beat – beaten)
Proofreading tip

Underline every verb in your summary. Is it in the present?

Verb forms: -s, modal verbs, if-clauses

-s and agreement: more than half of youmodal verbs and if-clauses: about a third of you

How it works

  1. he, she, it: -s, does, has
  2. Find the real subject: The possibilities of … are
  3. Modal verb + base form: can speed, might be
  4. No would in the if-clause

    Words you must get right

    spelling mistakes in every exam These came up again and again. How do you spell them correctly? Guess first, then turn the card.

    Remember

    Names and titles are on the task sheet: Gibberne, the New Accelerator, H. G. Wells. Copy them correctly.

    Endings: -ies, -ves, -ed

    plural endings: more than half of you-ed: almost half of you

    How it works

    1. Consonant + y → -ies (but vowel + y: days, ways)
    2. f / fe → -ves: lives, halves
    3. Regular verbs: always -ed, never -d or -t
    And

    No apostrophe in the plural: possibilities, not possibility’s.

    Capital letters: English is not German

    in almost every exam English nouns are small. But some words are always capitalised.

    How it works

    1. Nouns are small: text, narrator, humans, generation
    2. Nationalities and names: capital (Americans, Native Americans, Soviet)
    3. Titles: capitals for the important words; short stories and speeches in quotation marks
    4. Title + name: Professor Gibberne – but: the professor

      Apostrophes

      in two out of three exams Apostrophes were missing where they are needed and appeared where they are not allowed.

      How it works

      1. it’s = it is · its = possessive
      2. Plural: no apostrophe (humans, possibilities)
      3. Verbs: no apostrophe (feels, starts, lets)
      4. Possession: Turner’s, the narrator’s, the settlers’
      5. Formal text: full forms (cannot, does not, it is)

      Commas: English rules, not German ones

      commas too many: most of youcommas missing: everyone English needs fewer commas than German – but in other places.

      How it works

      1. No comma before that (if, what, how)
      2. No comma between subject and verb
      3. Defining relative clause: no comma
      4. Extra information: two commas
      5. After introductory words and clauses: comma

      Where does a sentence end?

      two main clauses with a comma: half of yousentences without a main clause: more than half of you

      How it works

      1. Two main clauses: full stop, semicolon or linking word – not just a comma
      2. Every sentence needs a subject and a verb
      3. because, although and if clauses cannot stand alone

        Articles and uncountable nouns

        articles: almost everyoneuncountable nouns: half of you

        How it works

        1. General meaning: no article (society, people, human progress, technology)
        2. Uncountable: no a, no plural (progress, information, homework)
        3. a or an: it depends on the sound
        4. fewer + countable · less + uncountable

        Adverbs, word order, pronouns

        adverbs: two out of threeword order: more than halfpronouns: half of you

        How it works

        1. How? → adverb (slowly, quickly, well)
        2. also and not after the first auxiliary
        3. After Moreover or Furthermore: normal word order
        4. Pronouns must match: technologies → they

              The discussion: a clear structure

              Task: Discuss whether overcoming human limitations through science and technology should be considered a new frontier of human progress.

              Introduction D1

              1. Lead-inToday, technology promises to make us faster, smarter and perhaps even immortal.
              2. The question in your own wordsBut should pushing our human limits really be seen as a new frontier of progress?
              3. Explain “frontier” as an imageFor Turner, the frontier was the line between settled land and the wilderness that Americans kept pushing west. Today, the limits of the human body and mind could be such a frontier.
              4. Your criterion for progressIn my view, a development is only progress if it makes life better and safer for many people, not just faster for a few.
              ▼

              Main part D2 · D3 · D4 · D5

              • Arguments for – two or three, each one developed
              • Arguments against – two or three, developed; include the responsibility for the consequences
              • Evidence from the extract and from at least one work from class
              ▼

              Weighing D6

              Which side is stronger – and why? Use your criterion from the introduction.

              ▼

              Conclusion D6

              A clear answer to the question of the task. No new arguments – and not just “there are pros and cons”.

              From a point to an argument

              Many arguments were only named, not developed. A named argument gets one point at most.

              ✗
              Another disadvantage is crime.
              only named – no explanation, no evidence
              ClaimOne serious risk is that a drug like the Accelerator could be used for crimes.
              ExplanationSince its users would move a thousand times faster than everyone else, nobody could see them, let alone stop them.
              EvidenceWells’ narrator even admits that crimes could be committed “with impunity”, yet he and Gibberne decide to sell the drug anyway.
              LinkThis shows that overcoming human limits is only progress if someone takes responsibility for the consequences.
              Pattern
              Claim+Explanation+Evidence+Link to the question

              Evidence: the extract (precise!) or one of the works from class.

              Using the extract as evidence

              text work: the weakest part The ending of the extract is your strongest evidence – and hardly anyone used it.

              VaguePrecise
              ✗As in the story, criminals could use it.
              ✓The narrator himself admits that the drug would allow crimes to be committed “with impunity”.Who says it? What exactly does the text say?
              ✗The text shows that the drug makes people happy.
              ✓Looking at the frozen crowd, the narrator feels “an exultant sense of superior advantage” – the drug makes him feel superior to others.Quote or precise paraphrase – then explain what it shows.
              ✗Wells thinks that selling the drug is fine.
              ✓The two men decide to sell the drug and leave the consequences to others: “as for the consequences – we shall see.” This careless ending suggests that the text criticises their attitude.Keep apart: what the characters think – what the text suggests – what you think.
              Useful phrases
              As the narrator admits, …Gibberne claims that …The ending shows that …This suggests that …Unlike the characters, I think …

              Weighing and conclusion

              weighing and judgement: less than half of the points on average The end of your discussion must answer the question.

              CriterionIf progress means a better and safer life for many people, …
              Weighing… the risks outweigh the benefits, because a drug like the Accelerator would give a few people power over everyone else.
              JudgementTherefore, overcoming human limitations is a new frontier of progress only if its consequences can be controlled.
              Useful phrases
              The strongest argument is …Although …, … is more important.… outweigh(s) …On balance, …In conclusion, I believe that …

              No new arguments in the conclusion.

                Before you hand in: your checklist

                Plan ten minutes for proofreading. Read your text several times – each time with one question in mind.

                1. VerbsSummary in the simple present? -s with he, she, it? Base form after modal verbs?
                2. SpellingNames and title copied correctly? -ies, -ves, -ed?
                3. Capital lettersNouns small? Nationalities, names and titles capital?
                4. Apostrophesit’s or its? No apostrophe in plurals and verbs?
                5. Commas and full stopsNo comma before that or between subject and verb? Every sentence with subject and verb?
                6. Wordscommit crimes, the drug, the first-person narrator, pros and cons – no German words?
                7. SummaryText type, title, author, year, topic? Own words, no quotes, no opinion – and the ending?
                8. Discussion“Frontier” explained, criterion, developed arguments with evidence, weighing, clear judgement?
